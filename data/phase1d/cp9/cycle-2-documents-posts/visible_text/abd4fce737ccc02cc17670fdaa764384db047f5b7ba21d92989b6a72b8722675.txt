Good News and Bad News
Good News and Bad News:
I will start with the Bad News:
When I first started planning on scaling production I found a Robotic Welding company VC backed with big, big money (that shall remain nameless for legal reasons and here after be called Roby) that met with us many many times and was convinced that they could build a large scale robotic welder that would completely weld together our trailers. We would tack them together, then presto, in less than a day we would have a robotically welded together assembly then a full trailer. Many, many meetings with our engineers and their engineers. A contract was signed, it was great because it had a low monthly cost and would allow the entire production to scale. Our production scaling was based around this process. The robot was late, became even later, meanwhile we are getting further behind. I didn't want to invest a lot of money in traditional manufacturing knowing this robot was coming. I won't go into all of the dirty details, but eventually when pressed they admitted the robot just couldn't do it.
This means that our entire production was vastly slowed, and 2022 wasn't any better than 2021. Other delays happened like the new laser wasn't making parts until April, We are perpetually late on delivering because of this and generally do our best to catch up. The backlog still stood at around $2M in April.
Summary on the bad news: I blundered in believing in Roby, putting us a year back and irritating customers.
Good News:
1) The backlog is now around $1.5M with new orders still coming in despite our dropping down to 1 salesperson. In addition a hard shift has occurred to move back to non-Roby based manufacturing with 6 new welders hired, more people in fabrication, more staff in engineering. More welding machines. The laser is cranking away making parts. The new press is cranking away bending parts, the new crane is scheduled to arrive in August (8 months late). The main fabrication floor is getting redesigned away from the Roby model and into a more traditional manufacturing model. We look likely to get out enough units to drop our backlog from 200 days to 90 days and then some more drop. We are looking to bring in another salesperson.
2) Phillip Reynolds has joined the team as our new CFO. Phillip was brought in by the Board of Directors of our neighbor startup in the industrial park and in 4 years was instrumental in that company selling for $230M.
3) We are going to do another Republic crowd funding raise.
Summary Good News: All of that equipment we bought is finally arriving, starting to make parts and fabrication is currently 2x and accelerating. Sales are still up. We have started putting together the team to build the company up for a 4 or 5 year acquisition.
Overall summary:
I appologize for the giant blunder. I really believed that the company Roby could build the robot, I met with them every week, visited their plant numerous times, I think that they thought that they could do it. But still there were clues. Like when we would send assemblies up to their plant to be welded we were under the impression that they were testing the robot. When we got them back it was clear they weren't robotically welded and instead Roby had hired a large crew to hand weld things together. At that point we really started pressing them which finally led to the admission that they couldn't do it. I probably should have pressed harder and sooner.
Now that we have shifted back to where we should have been a year ago things are really starting to accelerate.
Sean Jones
