Hi Ray, It's great to hear how quickly things are progressing!  Looking forward to the next phase of AlphaFlow's growth.

Thanks!  Erik

Ray Sturm
Hi Erik - Fantastic question. I started my career at Bear Stearns and was there when things fell apart in 2008, so I think I have always had the potential of a drastic shift as part of my professional DNA. Our average LTV is almost exactly 70%, so we've stayed fairly conservative in the types of loans we do. While we don't have capital at risk ourselves, the success of our investors on our platform will ultimately tie into their stickiness to our platform and thus our long term value. As such, we've focused more on "singles and doubles" than "home runs" in our space. While the market is hot now, our whole industry froze last year and delinquencies skyrocketed in the months after the pandemic hit. We were able to work our portfolio back to health far ahead of many in the industry because (1) our focus on experienced borrowers with conservative loan terms, and (2) our approach of solving for exits vs payments (see my colleague's great piece here:
linkedin.com/pulse/successful-lenders-solve-exits-payments-chase-scott
), which makes you an ally to lenders / borrowers when things get tough. Zooming out, the way we think about this starts with looking at each property at the zip code level and not only looking at how the market is doing today, but also looking to stress test it vs the biggest 12 and 18 month drop in prices in that zip code in the last 30 years (almost always between 2007 and 2009) and making sure we are still in the black. Finally, we bring more options than ever to the table these days. If a true bear market strikes, rentals typically go up and we have a healthy rental loan product and market to transition bridge loans to rental loans and ride out a storm. Apologies for the long answer but we think about this a LOT and it's a fundamental real estate investment question! Love it and appreciate you asking!
