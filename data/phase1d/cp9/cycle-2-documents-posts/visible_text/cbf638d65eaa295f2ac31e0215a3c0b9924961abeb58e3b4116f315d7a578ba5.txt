Still here!
I have recently realized that I did some updates on our 2nd campaign but have not done anything on this one. I am very sorry about that. It has been a turbulent several years. By the time we finally got our facility put together we went from not being able to fill orders to the freight market crash and recession. The order book evaporated. More recently the trade wars made everything much more expsensive to both buy and sell. 20% of our orders were to Canada and all of those dissappeared.
We are still making and selling QuickLoadz, but starting in 2024 we partnered with Horizon Motor to build EV trucks. Horizon Motor did a nationwide search and found QuickLoadz. We were a perfect fit in that we have expertise in EV, we build our own batteries for our trailers, heavy manufacturing, and lots of skilled people with lots of fancy equipment. The work was very sucessful. Other EV truck manfacturers burned through 100's of millions to billions of dollars and never delivered and often never got anything actually built. QuickLoadz set a record from start to EPA, CARB, HVIP approvals then building trucks and now delivering trucks. The model was extremely low capital, entirely relying on QuickLoadz existing infrastructure to pull it off. There are a lot of orders, there are trucks getting finished and delivered.
It is very exciting.
Sean Jones
QuickLoadz

Sean Jones
@adam-sampson We are stable with a small positive EBITA for the last 2 years, This was expected as we focused on the EV trucks. Now that we are actually shipping trucks and delivering we expect to get to a fairly strong EBITA by the end of 2027 and likely will be an aquisition target. In addition once we get some more time and cash in hand I plan to really start pushing our primary product of QuickLoadz again. We haven't really been marketing Qlz because it requires so much time to build. We have realized that the plan to build it as a vertical monopoly raw steel to finished product was very difficult without a really large plant and investment. Then you are caught in the place of trying to pair demand with capital sitting in a plant. The reason things moved so fast with the EV trucks is we make very little of that ourselves and so have very little capital outlay and are primarily assemblers. For QuickLoadz we really need to reassess the deeper costs associated with making everything ourselves. Sorry for the overlong answer.
